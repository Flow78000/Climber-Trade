Best Prop Firms in 2026
Honest Comparison for Serious Traders

Published March 7, 202614 min read

The prop firm industry has exploded. There are now dozens of firms competing for traders, each claiming to offer the best evaluation, the highest splits, and the most capital. Sorting the legitimate operations from the marketing machines requires a framework. This article provides one.

Important disclosure: this article is published by Climber Trade, which means we have a perspective. We have tried to be genuinely objective, and where we discuss our own model, we label it clearly. Your due diligence should extend beyond any single source, including this one.

The Evaluation Criteria

We evaluate firms across six dimensions that matter most to traders who want to build a long-term funded career:

  1. Evaluation fairness: Is the evaluation designed for you to pass or to fail?
  2. Capital transparency: Can you verify the firm has real capital to deploy?
  3. Instrument breadth: How many markets can you trade?
  4. Profit split value: What is your effective take-home after all factors?
  5. Payment reliability: Does the firm actually pay its funded traders?
  6. Scaling potential: Can your allocation grow over time?

Category 1: Challenge-Based Firms

The Model

Challenge-based firms set a fixed profit target (typically 8-10%) with a maximum drawdown limit (5-10%) within a defined time window (usually 30 days for phase 1, 60 days for phase 2). Pass both phases and you get funded. Fail and you pay again.

Strengths

  • Simple and easy to understand
  • Fast path to funding for skilled, aggressive traders
  • Well-established model with large user base and community feedback
  • Some firms offer high headline profit splits (80/20 or 90/10)

Weaknesses

  • Fixed profit targets encourage risk-seeking behavior
  • Time pressure creates artificial urgency
  • Binary pass/fail provides no gradated feedback
  • High failure rates (industry estimates suggest 85-95% fail)
  • Revenue model is primarily driven by re-subscription from failed traders

Firms in this category include FTMO, MyFundedFX, and The5ers (in their challenge products). They work well for traders who can handle pressure and have very high-conviction strategies. They work poorly for traders who need time to demonstrate consistency.

Category 2: Combine/Step-Based Firms

The Model

Similar to challenges but structured as a multi-step combine with escalating requirements. Some firms (like TopStep) focus specifically on futures. Traders must meet profit targets while respecting trailing drawdown limits across multiple steps.

Strengths

  • Futures-specific firms understand the asset class deeply
  • Some offer more generous drawdown limits than challenge firms
  • Community and educational resources are often strong

Weaknesses

  • Same fundamental problem: fixed targets create artificial trading behavior
  • Trailing drawdown mechanics can be confusing and punitive
  • Capital allocation is often fixed rather than dynamic
  • Limited to futures in many cases

Category 3: Investor-Matching Platforms

The Model

Pioneered by Darwinex, this model connects traders with investors. Traders build a track record, and investors allocate capital to the strategies they find most attractive. The platform takes a fee from the investor's allocation.

Strengths

  • No artificial evaluation targets
  • Track record can attract significant capital from multiple investors
  • True market-based allocation (capital flows to the best traders)

Weaknesses

  • Dependence on investor sentiment and flows
  • Building a track record from zero can take 6-12 months before attracting capital
  • Investor withdrawal can reduce your allocation unexpectedly
  • Complexity of the platform can be overwhelming for beginners

Category 4: Score-Based Continuous Evaluation

The Model

This is the model Climber Trade uses. There are no profit targets and no time limits. Instead, every trade contributes to a continuous composite score (the CLIMB Score) that evaluates risk management, performance, consistency, and execution quality. Funding is triggered when the score reaches 85 and is maintained for 7 consecutive trading days.

Strengths

  • No artificial targets that distort trading behavior
  • Continuous feedback shows exactly where your trading is strong or weak
  • Dynamic profit splits that improve as your score improves
  • Multi-asset: futures, forex, equities, CFDs, crypto (BTC/ETH)
  • Transparent liquidity pool with public dashboard
  • Capital allocation scales continuously with score and seniority

Weaknesses

  • More complex scoring system requires study to understand
  • No fixed funding guarantee (allocation depends on pool size and cohort)
  • Newer model with less industry track record than legacy firms
  • Initial capital allocations are modest until score and seniority build

What Matters Most: The Transparency Test

Regardless of category, the single most reliable indicator of a firm's legitimacy is transparency. Ask these questions:

  • Can I see how much capital the firm has? If there is no public pool or auditable capital disclosure, you are trusting a marketing claim.
  • Can I verify that traders receive payouts? Look for independent payment proofs in community forums, not the firm's own social media.
  • Are the rules clear before I pay? Hidden clauses that appear only in the fine print are a red flag. Read the trading rules and contract before committing.
  • Does the firm profit from my success or my failure? If the primary revenue is re-subscription from failed traders, the incentive is to make the evaluation difficult to pass, not to fund good traders.

Our Recommendation Framework

There is no single best prop firm for everyone. The best firm depends on your trading style, instrument preferences, risk tolerance, and timeline:

  • If you are aggressive and want fast funding: A challenge-based firm with generous drawdown limits may suit you.
  • If you trade exclusively futures: A futures-focused combine firm offers the deepest understanding of your market.
  • If you value transparency and long-term scaling: A score-based model with a public capital pool provides the most alignment.
  • If you want investor capital with no time pressure: An investor-matching platform lets the market decide.

The worst choice is picking a firm based on price alone or based on influencer recommendations driven by affiliate commissions. Do your research. Read the contracts. Verify the payouts. Then trade.
