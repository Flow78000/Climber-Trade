Best Futures Contracts to Trade in 2026: ES, NQ, CL, GC Compared

A practical comparison of the four most traded futures contracts, with margin data, tick values, volatility profiles, and micro contract alternatives.

12 min read March 2026

Choosing which futures contract to trade is one of the most important decisions you will make as a futures trader. Each contract has its own personality: different volatility profiles, different margin requirements, different optimal trading hours, and different relationships to economic data. The right contract for you depends on your account size, risk tolerance, trading style, and the time of day you are available to trade.

This guide compares the four most popular futures contracts among retail traders: ES (S&P 500), NQ (Nasdaq-100), CL (Crude Oil), and GC (Gold). We include their micro contract alternatives for traders who want smaller position sizes.

ES: E-mini S&P 500 (CME)

Contract specifications

  • Multiplier: $50 per index point
  • Tick size: 0.25 points
  • Tick value: $12.50
  • Notional value: Approximately $260,000 (at S&P 5,200)
  • Exchange initial margin: Approximately $12,650 (subject to change)
  • Day trading margin (broker): Often $500 to $2,000 depending on the broker

Why traders choose ES

ES is the most liquid futures contract in the world. On a typical day, millions of contracts change hands. This deep liquidity means tight bid-ask spreads (usually one tick), fast fills, and minimal slippage even on larger orders. ES is the benchmark against which all other index futures are measured.

The volatility profile is moderate and consistent. Average daily ranges vary with market conditions, but ES typically moves between 30 and 80 points during Regular Trading Hours. This creates enough movement for day trading while being manageable for risk management purposes.

Best trading hours

The most active period is the US Regular Trading Hours session: 9:30 AM to 4:15 PM Eastern. Within that, the first 90 minutes (9:30 to 11:00 AM) and the last hour (3:00 to 4:00 PM) typically see the highest volume and most directional movement. The period between 11:30 AM and 2:00 PM often sees reduced activity and choppy, range-bound price action.

Who should trade ES

ES suits almost every trading style. Scalpers benefit from the tight spreads and deep liquidity. Swing traders benefit from the smooth, trend-following behavior during directional moves. Beginners benefit from the predictability and the massive amount of educational content available. If you can only trade one futures contract, ES is the default choice.

NQ: E-mini Nasdaq-100 (CME)

Contract specifications

  • Multiplier: $20 per index point
  • Tick size: 0.25 points
  • Tick value: $5.00
  • Notional value: Approximately $370,000 (at Nasdaq 18,500)
  • Exchange initial margin: Approximately $18,400 (subject to change)
  • Day trading margin (broker): Often $1,000 to $3,000

Why traders choose NQ

NQ offers significantly more volatility than ES. The Nasdaq-100 is heavily weighted toward technology stocks, which tend to make larger moves. On a typical day, NQ can move 150 to 400 points, compared to ES's 30 to 80 points. Despite the smaller per-tick value ($5.00 vs. $12.50 for ES), the larger daily range means NQ often produces larger dollar moves per contract per day.

This volatility is a double-edged sword. Larger moves mean larger potential profits but also larger potential losses. Risk management discipline is even more important with NQ than with ES. The contract rewards traders who can identify momentum and trend direction, but it punishes those who fight the trend or overtrade.

Best trading hours

Like ES, the prime window is 9:30 AM to 11:00 AM Eastern, when the market open creates strong directional moves. NQ is particularly reactive to technology earnings reports (before and after market hours) and to FOMC announcements. The overnight session can be active when Asian or European technology news drives price movement.

Who should trade NQ

NQ appeals to momentum traders and those comfortable with larger intraday swings. The smaller tick value ($5.00) provides more granular position sizing compared to ES, which can help with risk management on smaller accounts. However, the higher volatility means NQ is generally not the best starting point for complete beginners.

CL: Crude Oil (NYMEX)

Contract specifications

  • Multiplier: 1,000 barrels ($1,000 per dollar move)
  • Tick size: $0.01
  • Tick value: $10.00
  • Notional value: Approximately $70,000 (at $70/barrel)
  • Exchange initial margin: Approximately $6,500 (subject to change)
  • Day trading margin (broker): Often $500 to $1,500

Why traders choose CL

Crude oil futures offer a completely different market dynamic than equity index futures. Oil prices are driven by supply and demand fundamentals, OPEC decisions, geopolitical events, inventory reports, and seasonal patterns. This creates opportunities that are uncorrelated with stock market movements, which is valuable for portfolio diversification.

CL is highly liquid during its most active hours and provides excellent volatility for day trading. Weekly inventory reports (Wednesday at 10:30 AM ET) often create sharp moves that skilled traders can capitalize on. The lower notional value compared to ES or NQ makes CL accessible with smaller accounts.

Best trading hours

CL is most active during the US session, particularly from 9:00 AM to 2:30 PM Eastern. The Wednesday EIA inventory report at 10:30 AM ET is the single most important weekly event for crude oil traders. The first hour of the US session (9:00 to 10:00 AM) often sets the directional tone for the day.

Who should trade CL

Traders who enjoy following fundamental drivers (geopolitics, supply data, seasonal patterns) and who want exposure to a non-equity market. CL requires understanding of energy market catalysts that do not affect equity indices. It pairs well with ES or NQ for traders who want to diversify across market types.

GC: Gold (COMEX)

Contract specifications

  • Multiplier: 100 troy ounces
  • Tick size: $0.10
  • Tick value: $10.00
  • Notional value: Approximately $200,000 (at $2,000/oz)
  • Exchange initial margin: Approximately $10,000 (subject to change)
  • Day trading margin (broker): Often $1,000 to $2,500

Why traders choose GC

Gold is one of the most widely traded commodities in the world. It responds to interest rate expectations, US dollar strength, inflation data, and global risk sentiment. During periods of market uncertainty, gold often moves in the opposite direction of equities, making it a natural hedge and diversification tool.

GC provides moderate, manageable volatility with good liquidity during active hours. The average daily range is typically $15 to $40 (150 to 400 ticks at $10 each), which provides ample opportunity for day traders while being more predictable than NQ or CL on most days.

Best trading hours

Gold is active during both the European session (3:00 AM to 8:00 AM ET) and the US session (8:30 AM to 1:30 PM ET). The overlap between these sessions (8:30 to 11:30 AM ET) often produces the strongest moves. Gold is particularly reactive to Federal Reserve communications, Non-Farm Payroll reports, and CPI data releases.

Who should trade GC

Traders who want a market that responds to macroeconomic themes rather than corporate earnings. Gold suits traders who follow central bank policy and global risk sentiment. The European session activity also makes GC attractive for traders in time zones where US Regular Trading Hours are inconvenient.

Micro Contracts: Smaller Size, Same Markets

Every contract above has a micro equivalent that trades at one-tenth the standard size:

  • MES: $5/point, $1.25/tick. The ideal starting point for futures trading.
  • MNQ: $2/point, $0.50/tick. Access Nasdaq volatility with minimal capital.
  • MCL: 100 barrels, $1.00/tick. Oil trading without full-size risk.
  • MGC: 10 oz, $1.00/tick. Gold exposure at beginner-friendly size.

Micro contracts are available on the same exchanges and trade during the same hours as their full-size equivalents. Liquidity on micros has grown substantially since their introduction and is more than sufficient for retail trading. Choosing Your Contract: A Decision Framework

By account size

  • Under $10,000: Start with micro contracts (MES, MNQ). The smaller tick values keep individual trade risk manageable.
  • $10,000–$50,000: Full-size ES or CL, or multiple micro contracts for more precise sizing.
  • $50,000+: Full-size contracts including NQ and GC, with micro contracts for fine-tuning position size.

By trading style

  • Scalping (seconds to minutes): ES for liquidity and tight spreads. NQ for larger tick moves.
  • Day trading (minutes to hours): Any of the four, depending on your market preference and available trading hours.
  • Swing trading (days to weeks): GC or ES for more controlled overnight behavior. CL requires caution due to inventory-driven gaps.

By available trading hours

  • US market hours (9:30 AM–4:00 PM ET): ES and NQ are ideal.
  • European hours (3:00 AM–11:30 AM ET): GC and European index futures (FDAX, FESX).
  • Asian hours (7:00 PM–3:00 AM ET): Lower volume across all four. Consider markets like the Nikkei 225 futures or stick to micro contracts for reduced risk.

What to Evaluate When Choosing

Before committing to a contract, spend at least two weeks observing it during your intended trading hours. Watch the price action, note the average daily range, observe how it reacts to economic data, and assess whether the volatility profile matches your risk tolerance. Understanding leverage is critical here: the notional value formula (multiplier multiplied by price) tells you your true exposure. A prop firm evaluation framework like the CLIMB Score will assess your leverage usage, so keeping intraday leverage in the optimal 2x-5x range matters for your score.

Do not choose a contract based solely on how much money it can make. Choose based on which market you understand, which volatility profile matches your temperament, and which trading hours fit your lifestyle. Consistency over months matters far more than one spectacular day.

The best futures contract is the one you understand deeply enough to trade with discipline, not the one with the largest daily range.
