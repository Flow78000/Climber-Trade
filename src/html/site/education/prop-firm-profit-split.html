Prop Firm Profit Splits Explained: Fixed vs Dynamic Models

How profit sharing works across the industry, why the structure matters more than the headline number, and how dynamic splits align firm and trader incentives.

9 min read March 2026

Profit splits are one of the first things traders look at when comparing prop firms. A firm advertising 90/10 in the trader's favor sounds better than one offering 80/20, and certainly better than 50/50. But the headline number is only part of the story. The structure of the split, whether it is fixed or dynamic, whether there are conditions attached, and how it interacts with capital allocation, matters just as much as the percentage itself.

What Is a Profit Split?

A profit split defines how trading profits are divided between the trader and the prop firm. If you generate $10,000 in profits on your funded account and the split is 80/20, you receive $8,000 and the firm keeps $2,000. Simple in concept, but the details vary significantly across firms.

The split compensates the firm for providing capital, infrastructure, risk management, and operational support. Without a viable split, firms cannot sustain operations, which means they cannot fund traders. The split is not a tax on your profits. It is the economic foundation that makes funded trading possible.

Fixed Splits: The Traditional Model

Most prop firms use fixed profit splits. Once you are funded, you receive the same percentage regardless of performance. Common structures include:

  • 90/10: Increasingly common as a marketing tool. The trader keeps 90% of profits.
  • 80/20: The industry standard for many years. A balanced split that funds firm operations.
  • 70/30 or 75/25: Less common but seen in firms that offer larger capital allocations or additional services.

Advantages of fixed splits

Simplicity is the main benefit. You know exactly what you will receive from day one, and the calculation never changes. This makes financial planning straightforward. There is no ambiguity and no performance-dependent variability in your payout percentage.

Limitations of fixed splits

Fixed splits create a static relationship. Whether you are the best trader on the platform or barely maintaining the minimum requirements, your percentage stays the same. There is no additional reward for improvement and no mechanism for the split to reflect your evolving contribution. High-performing traders subsidize the risk that lower-performing traders introduce, receiving the same split despite creating disproportionately more value.

Fixed splits also invite a race to the bottom. When firms compete on split percentage alone, the numbers become marketing tools rather than reflections of sustainable economics. A firm offering 95/5 must make revenue somewhere else, often through evaluation fees from traders who never reach funded status, hidden costs, or restrictions that reduce actual payouts.

Dynamic Splits: Performance-Based Sharing

Dynamic profit splits adjust based on measurable performance criteria. Instead of a flat percentage, the split improves as the trader demonstrates better results. This model is newer in the prop firm industry but draws from how institutional trading desks have compensated their traders for decades.

At Climber Trade, for example, the profit split scales with the CLIMB Score:

  • Score 85–89 (Standard): 50/50 split
  • Score 90–94 (Expert): 55/45 split
  • Score 95–100 (Elite): 60/40 split

The split is not fixed at funding. It evolves in real time as your trading quality changes. Score higher, earn a bigger share. If your score drops, the split adjusts downward, reflecting the increased risk your trading represents to the capital pool.

Advantages of dynamic splits

  • Continuous incentive. You always have a reason to improve your trading. There is no plateau where better performance yields the same reward.
  • Aligned incentives. The firm makes more money when you trade better, which means the firm is genuinely motivated to help you improve. Education, support, and analytics become profit centers for the firm, not cost centers.
  • Risk-appropriate sharing. Traders who manage risk well and produce consistent returns receive a larger share. Traders with riskier profiles receive less, which accurately reflects the risk they impose on the firm's capital.
  • Sustainable economics. Dynamic splits do not need to compete on headline percentages because the value proposition is the growth trajectory, not the starting number.

Considerations with dynamic splits

The starting split may appear lower than fixed-split competitors. A 50/50 starting point looks worse than a competitor's fixed 80/20 on a comparison table. However, if the dynamic model provides larger capital allocations, actual payouts may be equal or higher in practice. A 50% split on a larger allocation can produce more income than 80% of a smaller one.

Dynamic splits also require a transparent, well-documented scoring system. Traders need to trust that the scoring is fair, consistent, and based on objective metrics. Without transparency, a dynamic split could be perceived as a way for the firm to reduce payouts arbitrarily.

What Matters More Than the Percentage

Capital allocation

A 90% split on $25,000 of capital produces less income than a 50% split on $200,000. The split percentage is meaningless without knowing the capital you are trading. Firms that offer extremely generous splits often compensate by providing smaller account sizes. Always calculate expected income based on the combination of split and capital, not split alone.

Payout frequency and conditions

How often can you withdraw? Are there minimum profit requirements before your first withdrawal? Do you need to maintain the account for a specific period? Some firms with generous splits impose conditions that delay or reduce actual payouts. Check the withdrawal policies carefully.

Scaling potential

Does your capital allocation grow over time? A firm with a moderate split but strong scaling (automatic capital increases based on performance) may produce far more long-term income than a firm with a higher split but fixed capital. Monthly scaling, as offered through pool-based models, can compound capital significantly within the first year.

Sustainability of the firm

If a firm's economics do not work because the split is too generous, the firm will eventually fail or change its terms. A sustainable split that funds real operations, real capital, and real support creates a more reliable long-term partnership than an unsustainable headline number.

Common Traps to Avoid

  1. Comparing splits without comparing capital. Always look at the full picture: split percentage multiplied by available capital multiplied by realistic monthly return.
  2. Ignoring payout conditions. A 90% split means nothing if withdrawals are restricted, delayed, or subject to conditions that most traders cannot meet.
  3. Falling for promotional splits. Some firms offer 90/10 for the first month, then reduce to 70/30. Read the fine print.
  4. Overlooking the firm's revenue model. If you cannot understand how the firm makes money with the split they offer, the economics might not be real.

Which Model Is Right for You?

If you want predictability and simplicity, and you are confident in your ability to generate consistent profits at any performance level, a fixed split with a reputable firm works well. You know what you are getting from day one.

If you are growth-oriented and plan to continuously improve your trading, a dynamic split rewards that trajectory. The starting split may be lower, but the ceiling is higher, and the alignment of incentives means the firm is invested in your development, not just your subscription fees.

The best profit split is the one attached to real capital, fair rules, and a firm that wants you to succeed. The percentage matters less than the integrity behind it.
