Prop Firm Rules Decoded: What's Really Prohibited and Why

Understanding the reasoning behind prop firm restrictions — and why Climber Trade takes a different approach.

March 2026 · 10 min read

Why Rules Exist in the First Place

Every prop firm has rules. Some are straightforward (no insider trading), while others seem arbitrary (no trading during the first 5 minutes of the session, no holding through news, no more than 3 trades per day). Traders often view these restrictions as adversarial — designed to make passing harder and protect the firm's bottom line.

The reality is more nuanced. Rules exist to solve specific problems that prop firms face when allocating capital to external traders. Understanding the WHY behind each rule helps you evaluate which firms have genuine risk management concerns and which are using rules as a profit mechanism.

Copy Trading: Why It Is Universally Banned

Copy trading — replicating another trader's positions automatically across multiple accounts — is prohibited at virtually every prop firm. The reason is straightforward: it creates concentrated risk that the firm cannot manage.

The Risk Problem

If 50 traders are all copying the same signal provider, those 50 accounts are effectively one massive position. The firm believes it has 50 diversified traders with independent strategies, but it actually has 50 identical positions that will all profit or all lose simultaneously. A single bad call by the signal provider could wipe out all 50 accounts at once, creating a catastrophic loss event for the firm's capital pool.

The Evaluation Integrity Problem

Prop firm evaluations are designed to assess YOUR ability to trade. If you copy someone else's trades, the evaluation has measured their skill, not yours. When you reach live trading and the signal provider has a bad month — or simply stops providing signals — you have no ability to manage the capital independently. The firm has allocated real money based on false evidence of competence.

Climber Trade's Position

Copy trading between accounts is explicitly prohibited. This includes signal services, trade copiers, and coordinated entry/exit between Climber Trade accounts. The CLIMB Score is designed to evaluate individual trading ability. If your score was earned through someone else's signals, it does not reflect your actual capability and the capital allocation based on that score is not justified.

Latency Arbitrage: Exploiting Speed Rather Than Skill

Latency arbitrage exploits tiny price differences between data feeds or platforms that exist for fractions of a second. A trader with faster data can see price changes before the platform updates its quoted price, executing trades against stale prices for guaranteed profit.

Why It Is Banned

This is not trading — it is a technology exploit. The profits do not come from market analysis, risk management, or directional skill. They come from being faster than the platform's price feed. On a demo or evaluation account, latency arbitrage can generate impressive results that evaporate entirely on a live account where the exploit does not work against real market makers.

Firms that deploy real capital (like Climber Trade) ban latency arbitrage because positions generated this way cannot be replicated in live markets. A trader who "passes" via latency arbitrage would immediately lose money when switched to real capital execution.

Coordinated Trading and Market Manipulation

When multiple traders coordinate to enter the same position simultaneously, they can artificially move prices in illiquid markets. This is market manipulation — it is illegal in regulated markets and banned at every legitimate prop firm.

How It Works

A group of 20 funded traders agrees via a private chat to all buy NQ at exactly 10:15 AM. The concentrated buying pressure pushes the price up temporarily. They then sell into the momentum they created. On an individual basis, each trade looks like a normal directional bet. In aggregate, it is market manipulation.

Detection

Prop firms monitor for correlated entry times, identical instruments, and patterns that suggest coordination. At Climber Trade, the prohibition extends to any form of trading in a coordinated group — even if the intent is not manipulation, the concentration of risk and the inability to evaluate individual skill make it incompatible with the platform's model.

News Trading Restrictions at Other Firms

Many prop firms restrict or ban trading around major news events. Common restrictions include no new positions within 2 minutes of scheduled releases, mandatory closure of all positions before high-impact events, or automatic position liquidation at news time.

Why Other Firms Restrict It

News events create extreme volatility and liquidity gaps. On demo accounts, fills are often unrealistically clean — the simulation does not model the slippage, spread widening, and partial fills that happen in real markets during NFP or FOMC. A trader who consistently profits from news on a demo account may consistently lose on real capital due to execution differences.

Firms using simulated capital lose nothing when traders profit from unrealistic demo fills. But when that same strategy is expected to replicate on real capital, the execution gap becomes the firm's problem.

Climber Trade's Approach

News trading is fully allowed. Because Climber Trade uses Volumetrica as its trading platform and deploys real capital to live traders, the execution environment already reflects real market conditions. The CLIMB Score naturally handles news-related risk through its metrics — excessive daily volatility reduces your ABS_NPL score, and drawdowns from failed news trades impact your R_T_AFD metric. The scoring system IS the risk management framework, making additional news restrictions unnecessary.

Style Restrictions: Where Firms Differ Most

Some prop firms ban scalping (trades under 2 minutes), others ban swing trading (positions held overnight), and some ban both. These restrictions often reflect the firm's business model rather than legitimate risk concerns.

Why Some Firms Ban Scalping

Simulated-capital firms ban scalping because high-frequency, small-profit trades are the easiest way to exploit the difference between demo execution and real execution. A scalper taking 50 trades per day with a 1-point target benefits enormously from guaranteed fills at quoted prices. In reality, slippage would eliminate most of those profits. Banning scalping prevents traders from exploiting the demo environment.

Why Some Firms Ban Overnight Holds

Firms that cannot properly hedge their exposure ban overnight positions because gap risk is difficult to manage. If a trader holds a large NQ position overnight and an Asian session event causes a 3% gap, the firm absorbs the loss. Firms with proper risk management and real capital can handle this — firms running on thin margins from subscription fees cannot.

Rules should exist to protect the trading ecosystem, not to trap traders into failing. If a rule does not serve a clear risk management purpose, question why it exists.

Information Privileges and Insider Trading

This one needs no explanation — trading on material non-public information is illegal everywhere. Prop firms are legally obligated to prohibit it, and violations can result in criminal prosecution, not just account termination. If you have access to insider information, do not trade on it. Full stop.

How to Evaluate a Firm's Rules

When reviewing any prop firm's rulebook, ask these questions:

  1. Does the rule protect against genuine risk? Banning copy trading and market manipulation protects the firm and other traders. These are reasonable rules.
  2. Does the rule address an execution model limitation? Banning scalping or news trading often indicates the firm uses simulated capital where demo execution does not match reality. This is a business model limitation, not a risk management concern.
  3. Does the rule make the evaluation harder to pass? Rules that restrict the number of trades per day, mandate minimum hold times, or require specific trading hours make it harder to demonstrate your skill. Consider whether these restrictions serve the firm's interests more than yours.
  4. Are the consequences proportionate? A first-time minor violation should result in a warning, not immediate account termination. Firms with aggressive penalty structures may be using rule violations as a revenue mechanism.
