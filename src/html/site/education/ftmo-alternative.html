Looking for an FTMO Alternative?
Here's What to Consider in 2026

Published March 6, 202610 min read

FTMO is the most recognized name in prop trading. Founded in 2015, they essentially created the modern challenge-based evaluation model that dozens of competitors have since copied. Their brand recognition, community size, and track record are difficult to match. So why would anyone look for an alternative?

Because the challenge model, for all its simplicity, has structural limitations that do not serve every trader. And the industry has developed genuinely different approaches that address those limitations. This article explores when FTMO is the right choice, when it is not, and what alternatives exist for traders who need something different.

What FTMO Does Well

Credit where it is due. FTMO built a massive operation around a clear value proposition. Their strengths include:

  • Proven payout history. FTMO has paid out hundreds of millions to traders since launch. Payment reliability is not in question.
  • Large community. With tens of thousands of active traders, you can find reviews, strategies, and support easily.
  • Platform flexibility. Support for MT4, MT5, cTrader, and DXtrade gives traders platform choice.
  • Refundable fee. The evaluation fee is refundable upon your first profit split, which reduces the effective cost for traders who pass.

Where the Challenge Model Falls Short

The limitations are not specific to FTMO. They are inherent to any fixed-target, time-limited challenge:

Profit Targets Create Perverse Incentives

When you need to earn 10% within 30 days, the math pushes you toward aggressive trading. A conservative trader who earns 0.3% per day needs 33 trading days to reach the target. But there are only about 22 trading days in a month. This means conservative strategies are structurally disadvantaged in challenge-based evaluations, even though conservative strategies are often the most sustainable long-term.

Time Pressure Distorts Behavior

Day 25 of a challenge with 6% earned creates a fundamentally different psychological state than day 1. The temptation to take larger positions to close the remaining gap is enormous, and it causes traders to violate the exact risk management principles they will need when funded.

Binary Pass/Fail Wastes Information

A trader who earns 9.5% in a 10% challenge has failed. A trader who earns 10.1% has passed. The 0.6% difference between them is noise, not signal. Yet one gets funded and the other pays another fee. This binary outcome throws away all the nuanced information about trading quality that a more granular system would capture.

Failure Rate Is Not a Bug

Industry estimates suggest that 85-95% of challenge participants fail. The firms that operate this model earn the majority of their revenue from failed attempts and re-subscriptions. This creates an incentive structure where the firm profits most when traders fail, which is the opposite of aligned interests.

What Score-Based Models Do Differently

The continuous scoring approach, which Climber Trade uses through the CLIMB Score, addresses each of these limitations:

No Profit Target

There is no specific return you need to achieve. Instead, the quality of your trading is assessed continuously across risk management, performance, consistency, and execution quality. This means a trader earning 0.2% per day with excellent risk control can qualify just as readily as one earning 1% per day.

No Time Limit

The evaluation runs indefinitely on a monthly subscription. The minimum seasoning period is 30 days, but there is no maximum. This eliminates time pressure entirely and allows traders to demonstrate their edge at their natural pace.

Continuous Feedback

Instead of pass/fail, you receive a real-time score from 0 to 100 with detailed breakdowns by pillar and sub-metric. If your risk management is strong but your consistency is weak, you know exactly what to work on. This turns the evaluation from a gatekeeping exercise into a diagnostic and development tool.

Dynamic Splits and Allocation

Once funded, your profit split and capital allocation adjust dynamically based on your ongoing score. Improve from 85 to 95 and your split improves from 50/50 to 60/40 while your allocation increases. This creates continuous incentive to trade better, not just profitably enough to maintain funding.

Direct Comparison: Key Differences

Evaluation Structure

FTMO uses a two-phase challenge: Phase 1 requires 10% profit in 30 days, Phase 2 requires 5% in 60 days. Both have a 10% maximum loss limit and 5% daily loss limit. Climber Trade uses continuous scoring with a threshold of 85/100 maintained for 7 consecutive trading days, with a 30-day minimum seasoning period.

Instruments

FTMO focuses on forex and CFDs. Climber Trade offers futures (ES, NQ, CL, GC, and more), forex (28 pairs), US equities, CFDs, and crypto (BTC/ETH). If you trade futures, this is a significant differentiator.

Profit Splits

FTMO offers a fixed 80/20 split (upgradable to 90/10). Climber Trade offers dynamic splits from 50/50 to 60/40 based on score tier. The FTMO split is higher at the headline level, but the dynamic model at Climber Trade means your split improves as you prove yourself, and the capital allocation also scales, which can make the absolute earnings higher despite a lower percentage.

Capital Model

FTMO allocates fixed account sizes ($10K to $200K). Climber Trade allocates from a shared liquidity pool based on score, with no fixed ceiling. The pool model means your allocation can grow indefinitely as the pool grows and your score improves.

Transparency

FTMO does not publish pool or capital data. Climber Trade publishes a real-time liquidity pool dashboard accessible to everyone. This is perhaps the most fundamental difference: verifiable transparency versus trust-based claims.

When FTMO Is Still the Right Choice

FTMO remains a strong option if:

  • You trade primarily forex and are comfortable with the challenge format
  • You have a high-conviction strategy that can reliably hit 10% in 30 days without excessive risk
  • You value the established community and extensive third-party reviews
  • You prefer a fixed, high profit split over a dynamic one
  • You do not trade futures

When to Consider an Alternative

Look beyond FTMO if:

  • You have failed challenges repeatedly despite being a profitable trader on personal accounts
  • Your strategy is conservative and needs more than 30 days to demonstrate its edge
  • You trade futures and need a firm that supports CME/CBOT/NYMEX/COMEX contracts
  • You want continuous feedback on your trading quality, not just a pass/fail outcome
  • You want verified capital transparency, not marketing claims
  • You value dynamic profit splits that reward improvement over time

The prop firm industry is no longer one-size-fits-all. The traders who build the best careers are the ones who choose the model that matches their trading style, not the one with the biggest advertising budget.
