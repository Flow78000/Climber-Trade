TopStep vs Score-Based Prop Firms: A New Approach to Funded Trading

How the traditional combine model compares to comprehensive scoring, and what the shift means for traders seeking funded accounts in 2026.

11 min read March 2026

TopStep pioneered the modern prop firm evaluation model. Since its founding, it has helped thousands of futures traders prove their skills through a structured combine process with clear profit targets and drawdown limits. The model is straightforward, well understood, and has set the standard for the industry. But as the prop firm landscape evolves, a fundamentally different approach has emerged: score-based funding that evaluates traders across a comprehensive set of metrics rather than a single pass/fail profit target.

The Combine Model: How TopStep Works

TopStep's evaluation centers on the Trading Combine, a simulated account where traders must hit a specific profit target while staying within a maximum drawdown limit (both daily and trailing). Meet the target without breaching the drawdown, and you move forward to a funded account.

What makes the combine appealing

  • Clarity. The rules are binary: hit the profit target, stay within the drawdown. There is no ambiguity about what you need to achieve.
  • Speed. Skilled futures traders can pass a combine in days. There is no minimum evaluation period requiring months of data.
  • Futures focus. TopStep is built for futures traders and offers tight integration with major futures platforms. If you trade ES, NQ, or CL exclusively, the specialization is an advantage.
  • Established reputation. Years of operation, documented payouts, and a large community mean you are dealing with a known entity.

Where the combine model creates friction

The profit-target model evaluates one thing: can you generate X dollars within Y drawdown? It does not assess how you generate those profits. Two traders can pass the combine with identical P&L numbers while having vastly different risk profiles, consistency levels, and execution quality. One might be a disciplined, consistent operator while the other took oversized positions during a lucky news event.

The trailing drawdown mechanism also creates behavioral distortions. Traders who are close to the drawdown limit often stop trading altogether, waiting for time to pass rather than trading naturally. The combine rewards survival as much as skill, and the fixed profit target can push traders toward higher risk as deadlines approach.

The Score-Based Model: A Different Philosophy

Score-based evaluation replaces the single profit target with a comprehensive rating system. Climber Trade's CLIMB Score, for example, evaluates traders across 14 individual metrics organized into four weighted pillars:

  1. Risk Management (x4 weight) — drawdown control, leverage discipline, recovery time, daily P&L distribution
  2. Performance (x4 weight) — Profit Factor, Sharpe Ratio, annualized yield
  3. Consistency & Trading Style (x3 weight) — win rate, percentage of profitable days, lot size stability
  4. Quality Execution (multiplier) — skew, kurtosis, extreme performance ratios

There is no profit target. Instead, traders must achieve and maintain a score of 85 out of 100, held for seven consecutive trading days, with a minimum 30-day evaluation period. The score updates in real time, giving continuous feedback rather than a binary pass/fail at the end.

Key Differences That Matter

Single metric vs. holistic evaluation

The combine asks one question: did you make enough money without losing too much? The score-based model asks many questions: are you managing risk well, performing consistently, maintaining stable position sizing, and executing with statistical quality? This produces funded traders who are well-rounded rather than just profitable in a short window.

Asset class coverage

TopStep focuses exclusively on futures markets. Score-based platforms typically support multiple asset classes. The CLIMB Score framework applies equally to futures, forex, equities, and CFDs, giving traders who operate across markets a single evaluation path. If you trade NQ futures in the morning and EUR/USD in the evening, you do not need separate evaluations.

Feedback granularity

A combine gives you three numbers: your P&L, your drawdown, and whether you passed. A 14-metric score gives you specific, actionable information. Maybe your Profit Factor is strong but your Lot Stability Ratio is weak, meaning you vary position sizes too much. Maybe your win rate is high but your Sharpe Ratio is low, suggesting volatile returns despite frequent wins. This level of detail, available through the Climber Trade Academy, helps traders develop as professionals rather than just chase a number.

Dynamic profit splits vs. fixed splits

Most combine-based firms offer a fixed profit split (commonly 80/20 or 90/10). Score-based models often tie the split to your ongoing performance. At Climber Trade, the split ranges from 50/50 at the entry level to 60/40 for Elite traders scoring 95+, with the potential for further improvement through scaling. This creates an ongoing incentive to keep improving rather than coast once funded.

Time Pressure and Trading Psychology

The combine's profit target creates time pressure that does not exist in a score-based evaluation. Even though combines technically have generous timeframes, the monthly subscription cost creates psychological urgency. Traders feel pressure to hit the target quickly to avoid paying for another month.

Score-based evaluations eliminate this dynamic. There is no profit target to chase. Your job is simply to trade well across all dimensions. The evaluation rules focus on maintaining consistent quality rather than hitting a specific dollar amount. Many traders report that this shift reduces anxiety and produces more natural, disciplined trading behavior.

After the Evaluation: What Changes

Once funded through a combine, traders typically receive a fixed allocation with fixed rules. Score-based funding works differently. Your capital allocation from the liquidity pool scales dynamically with your score. Improve from 85 to 95, and both your allocation percentage and profit split improve. This creates a continuous growth trajectory rather than a static funded state.

Which Approach Should You Choose?

The combine model works well if:

  • You trade futures exclusively and do not need multi-asset evaluation.
  • You want a fast, clear-cut evaluation with a defined finish line.
  • You prefer a simple pass/fail framework over ongoing scoring.
  • You are confident in your ability to hit specific targets under time pressure.

Score-based funding works well if:

  • You trade multiple asset classes and want a unified evaluation.
  • You value detailed, continuous feedback on your trading quality.
  • You want capital allocation that grows with your performance over time.
  • You prefer an evaluation that rewards overall trading competence rather than short-term profit generation.

The Industry Trajectory

The prop firm industry is moving toward more sophisticated evaluation methods. The combine model was revolutionary when it launched, but the limitations of single-metric evaluation are becoming clearer as the industry matures. Score-based systems represent the next evolution, borrowing from how institutional trading desks actually evaluate their own traders: across multiple risk, performance, and behavioral dimensions simultaneously.

Neither model is objectively superior. They serve different trader profiles and different trading philosophies. The best choice is the one that aligns with how you actually trade and how you want to grow as a professional. Consider trying both approaches to determine which evaluation framework brings out your best trading.
