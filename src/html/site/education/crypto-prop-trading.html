Crypto Prop Trading in 2026: BTC and ETH on Funded Accounts

How to trade Bitcoin and Ethereum on a funded account without destroying your score.

March 2026 · 9 min read

The State of Crypto Prop Trading

Cryptocurrency has matured from a speculative curiosity into a legitimate asset class traded by institutions, hedge funds, and professional prop traders. The introduction of Bitcoin and Ethereum spot ETFs, the deepening of derivatives markets, and the growing correlation between crypto and macro events have all contributed to making crypto a standard component of professional trading portfolios.

For retail traders pursuing funded accounts, crypto offers unique advantages — high volatility creating frequent opportunities, extended trading hours, and strong technical patterns that complement traditional market analysis. However, the same volatility that creates opportunity also creates risk, and managing that risk within a prop firm framework requires specific strategies.

Which Prop Firms Offer Crypto?

Not all prop firms allow cryptocurrency trading. Many futures-only firms restrict instruments to CME-listed contracts, which limits crypto to BTC and ETH futures with specific margin requirements. Others operate entirely in the forex and CFD space and may offer broader crypto coverage but often with high spreads and limited liquidity.

CFD vs Futures: Understanding the Difference

Crypto CFDs and crypto futures are different instruments with different characteristics that affect how you should trade them on a funded account.

CFDs (Contracts for Difference)

CFDs track the underlying asset price without the complexity of contract expirations and rollovers. They offer flexible position sizing (you can trade fractional amounts), typically trade 24/7, and have lower initial margin requirements. The trade-off is that CFDs include spread and overnight financing costs that can erode returns on longer holds.

CME Futures

BTC and ETH futures on the CME offer institutional-grade liquidity and standardized contracts. BTC futures have a notional value of 5 BTC per contract, while Micro BTC futures are 1/10 of a Bitcoin. These trade during CME hours (Sunday-Friday with daily maintenance breaks) and have defined expiration dates. The advantage is tighter spreads during active hours and no overnight financing costs. The disadvantage is less flexible sizing and limited trading hours compared to CFDs.

24/7 Trading Considerations

One of crypto's defining features is continuous trading. Markets never close. This creates both opportunity and danger for funded traders.

The Opportunity

Significant crypto moves often happen outside traditional market hours — during Asian sessions, weekends, or late at night when traditional markets are closed. If you are a night trader or live in a time zone that does not align with US market hours, crypto gives you access to volatility when futures and equities are quiet.

The Danger

Twenty-four-hour access means twenty-four-hour exposure to temptation. The most common mistake among crypto-focused funded traders is overtrading. The market is always open, so there is always a chart to watch and always a setup that looks tradeable. This leads to excessive screen time, impulsive entries, and the erosion of the discipline that earned the funded account in the first place.

Setting Boundaries

Define your crypto trading hours the same way you would define your futures trading session. Perhaps you trade BTC during the New York overlap (8 AM to 12 PM Eastern) when liquidity is deepest, or during the Asian open when volatility spikes. Whatever window you choose, stick to it. Close your charts outside that window. Your trading journal should track when you violate your session rules and correlate those violations with performance.

Volatility Management for Crypto

BTC and ETH regularly move 3-5% in a single day and occasionally 10% or more. On a funded account where your CLIMB Score evaluates daily PnL through the ABS_NPL metric, this volatility requires careful position sizing.

Position Sizing Rules

The CLIMB Score awards the highest ABS_NPL points for daily returns between 0% and 1% of capital. Returns above 1% see diminishing scores, and large negative days are heavily penalized. With a $1M evaluation capital, this means your target daily PnL from crypto should stay within plus or minus $10,000.

If BTC moves 5% in a day, your position size needs to be small enough that this move stays within your risk budget. For a $1M account targeting a maximum daily loss of 1%, your maximum BTC exposure should be approximately $200,000 notional (1% / 5% potential move). This translates to roughly 2 BTC of exposure at current prices, well below the leverage limits that might be available.

Leverage Discipline

The Average Lot Number metric (CT4) in the CLIMB Score rewards leverage between 2x and 5x notional. Crypto's inherent volatility means you should stay at the lower end of this range when trading BTC and ETH. A 3x leveraged BTC position during a 5% daily move results in a 15% swing on your capital — catastrophic for your ABS_NPL score and potentially triggering automatic drawdown alerts.

Crypto volatility is your friend for opportunities but your enemy for risk metrics. Size down relative to what you would trade in less volatile instruments.

Combining Crypto With Futures

The most sophisticated funded traders use crypto as one component of a diversified approach rather than trading it in isolation.

Correlation Trading

BTC has developed meaningful correlations with macro assets. It often moves with risk-on sentiment (correlating with NQ), responds to dollar strength (inverse correlation with DXY), and reacts to interest rate expectations. These correlations create opportunities for relative value trades — going long BTC while hedging with a short position in a correlated but less volatile instrument, or using crypto as a leading indicator for equity moves.

Time Zone Arbitrage

Trade futures during US hours when liquidity is optimal and spreads are tightest. Switch to crypto during Asian or European sessions when equity futures are in maintenance windows. This approach maximizes your productive trading hours without the overlap and confusion of running both simultaneously.

Volatility Balancing

If your futures trading had a quiet day with minimal PnL, you might look for crypto setups to generate returns. Conversely, if you have already had a profitable futures session that puts you near your daily target, avoid adding crypto risk that could push your daily return above the 1% optimal zone. Think of your daily PnL as a budget and allocate it across instruments strategically.

Crypto-Specific Risk Factors

  • Weekend gaps: While crypto technically trades 24/7, liquidity drops significantly on weekends. The Climber Trade weekend position rule allows exposure up to 1:1 notional if your balance is above $50K. Keep weekend crypto positions small to avoid getting caught in a low-liquidity move.
  • Regulatory announcements: Crypto prices remain sensitive to regulatory news from the SEC, CFTC, and international bodies. These announcements are often unpredictable and can cause 5-10% moves within minutes. Treat them the same way you would treat high-impact news events — reduce size or stand aside.
  • Liquidity variations: BTC and ETH have vastly different liquidity profiles. BTC generally offers tighter spreads and deeper order books. ETH can have periods of significantly wider spreads, especially during volatile conditions. Factor spread costs into your expected returns.
  • Overnight financing: If trading crypto via CFDs, overnight financing costs accumulate daily. For swing positions held over multiple days, these costs can meaningfully reduce profitability. Calculate the break-even holding period for your position including financing costs before entering a multi-day crypto trade.

Building a Crypto Trading Plan for Your Funded Account

  1. Define your allocation: What percentage of your daily risk budget goes to crypto? Start small — perhaps 20-30% — and adjust as you build experience trading crypto within the CLIMB Score framework.
  2. Set session hours: Choose a 2-4 hour window for crypto trading and stick to it.
  3. Size conservatively: Target 2x-3x leverage maximum on crypto positions, even if higher leverage is available.
  4. Use stop losses religiously: Crypto can gap 2-3% in minutes. Always have a stop in place. Accept that slippage on stops is part of the cost of trading volatile instruments.
  5. Monitor correlations: Before entering a BTC trade, check what NQ, gold, and DXY are doing. Trading against a strong macro current is a common mistake in crypto.
