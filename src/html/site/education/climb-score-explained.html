CLIMB Score Explained
How Algorithmic Scoring Changes Everything

Published March 9, 202612 min read

The CLIMB Score is Climber Trade's proprietary scoring system that rates traders on a scale of 0 to 100. Unlike traditional pass/fail evaluations, the CLIMB Score provides a continuous, multi-dimensional assessment of your trading quality. It updates in real time and determines everything from your funding eligibility to your profit split and capital allocation.

This article explains every component of the score, how the math works, and how to optimize your trading to achieve and maintain the highest possible rating.

The Four Pillars

The CLIMB Score is built on four pillars, each measuring a different dimension of trading quality. The first three pillars are weighted and averaged, while the fourth acts as a multiplier that adjusts the final result.

The Formula

The final score calculation has two stages. First, a weighted average of the three primary pillars:

Weighted Average = [(Risk x 4) + (Performance x 4) + (Consistency x 3)] / 11

The denominator of 11 is simply the sum of the weights (4 + 4 + 3). Then, Quality Execution applies a multiplier that adjusts the average by up to 10% in either direction:

Final Score = Weighted Average x (1 + ((Quality Execution - 50) / 500))

If your Quality Execution score is 50 (neutral), the multiplier is exactly 1.0 and the average passes through unchanged. A QE score of 100 produces a multiplier of 1.10 (a 10% boost). A QE score of 0 produces a multiplier of 0.90 (a 10% reduction).

Pillar 1: Risk Management (Weight x4)

This is the most heavily weighted pillar because risk management is the strongest predictor of whether a trader will survive long-term. It contains four sub-metrics:

ABS_NPL: Daily Net Profit/Loss

This metric evaluates each trading day individually. The optimal daily return is around +1% of capital, which earns the maximum score of 100. Returns below 0% earn reduced scores (deeper losses earn lower scores), and returns above +1% also see diminishing scores because excessively profitable days often indicate excessive risk-taking.

This scoring curve is intentionally counterintuitive. It penalizes both large losses and large gains because both indicate volatility that is unsustainable. A trader who earns 0.3% on 20 days scores higher than one who earns 6% on one day, because the first pattern is repeatable while the second is not.

INPL: Initial Net PnL Drawdown

This tracks the peak-to-trough drawdown from your starting equity. Smaller drawdowns score higher. A drawdown beyond 10% of capital scores 0. The score remains reduced until your equity recovers to its previous peak, creating a persistent penalty for deep drawdowns.

R_T_AFD: Recovery Time After Drawdown

This measures how many trading days it takes to recover from each drawdown. The scoring is granular: a 0.5% drawdown recovered in 5 days scores 85, while the same drawdown recovered in 20 days scores only 39. Deeper drawdowns have stricter recovery expectations.

The practical implication: when you are in a drawdown, do not try to recover by increasing leverage. Reduce size, trade selectively, and prioritize getting back to breakeven through consistent small gains.

CT4: Average Leverage

Intraday leverage is scored on a curve where the optimal zone is 2x to 5x notional exposure. Below 2x is conservative and still scores well (1x = 80 points). Above 5x, the score drops rapidly: 10x = 60, 15x = 20, 18x+ = 0 or negative. Leverage above 25x earns -100 points, which can push the pillar score below zero.

This metric alone eliminates the over-leveraged gambling behavior that destroys most retail traders. If you want a high CLIMB Score, keep your leverage moderate. For a detailed analysis, read our leverage sweet spot guide.

Pillar 2: Performance (Weight x4)

Profit Factor

The ratio of total gross profits to total gross losses. A PF of 1.0 means you break even. PF of 1.5 means you earn $1.50 for every $1.00 lost. The score scales from 0 (PF below 0.50) to 100 (PF above 4.00), with a confidence adjustment based on trade count. You need at least 200 trades for the full confidence coefficient. Fewer trades means a discounted score.

Sharpe Ratio

The classic risk-adjusted return metric. In the CLIMB Score, the Sharpe Ratio acts as a bonus of up to 10 additional points, capping the total performance pillar at 100. A Sharpe above 2.0 earns about 8 bonus points. Above 2.9 earns the full 10. This rewards traders who generate returns with minimal volatility.

Annualized Yield

Your trading returns are annualized and compared to an 8% benchmark (the long-term market average). A score of 50/100 corresponds to matching the benchmark. Scoring 100/100 requires approximately 28% annualized returns. A confidence factor ramps up over 180 days, so early-stage returns are discounted.

Pillar 3: Consistency & Style (Weight x3)

Win Rate

Your percentage of winning trades, adjusted by a confidence factor that reaches full weight at 22 trading days. A 50% win rate scores 11 points. 60% scores 35. 70% scores 58. The curve penalizes extremely low win rates harshly (below 45% goes negative) while rewarding steady improvement.

Gain Days Ratio

Simply the percentage of days that end in profit. A trader profitable on 70% of trading days scores 70/100. This is a straightforward consistency metric that rewards traders who can show up and earn a positive PnL more days than not.

Lot Stability Ratio (LSR)

This measures how consistent your position sizing is. LSR = Average lot size / Standard deviation of lot sizes. An LSR of 4.0+ (very stable sizing) scores 100. Below 1.0 (highly variable sizing) scores 20 or less. Erratic position sizing is one of the clearest signals of undisciplined trading, and this metric catches it.

Pillar 4: Quality Execution (Multiplier)

This pillar does not have a weight. Instead, it produces a multiplier between 0.90 and 1.10 that adjusts the weighted average of the other three pillars. It contains four sub-metrics:

  • Skewness: Measures the asymmetry of your return distribution. Low skew (near 0) is ideal and scores 100.
  • Kurtosis: Measures the heaviness of the tails of your return distribution. Low kurtosis indicates few extreme outliers.
  • Extreme Performance Ratio: The ratio of your largest single-day result to your average daily result. Lower is better.
  • Best/Worst Day Ratio: Your best day divided by the absolute value of your worst day. Higher is better (best day should exceed worst day).

Collectively, these metrics reward traders whose profits come from a consistent edge rather than from occasional lucky outliers. A trader who earns small amounts consistently will score higher on QE than one who earns the same total through a few large wins and large losses.

Score Reliability Over Time

The CLIMB Score becomes more reliable as your trading history grows. Below 30 days, the score is marked as indicative. Between 30 and 120 days, it is considered fiable. Beyond 120 days, it is certified. The maximum confidence factor for yield calculations is reached at 180 days.

This means exceptional early performance is intentionally discounted relative to a longer track record. A trader with 200 days of solid data is valued more than one with 15 days of spectacular results.

How to Improve Your CLIMB Score

The most impactful actions, ranked by effect on overall score:

  1. Keep leverage between 2x and 5x. This directly affects the highest-weighted pillar.
  2. Limit daily losses. A single -3% day can drag your ABS_NPL score for weeks.
  3. Standardize position sizing. Use the same lot size for every trade to maximize your LSR.
  4. Trade consistently. Trade at least 4 days per month. More active trading with stable returns builds all metrics.
  5. Recover from drawdowns methodically. Reduce size, stay patient, and let the R_T_AFD metric reward your discipline.
