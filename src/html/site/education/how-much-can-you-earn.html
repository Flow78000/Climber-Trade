How Much Can You Really Earn
With a Prop Firm? Realistic Numbers

Published March 8, 20268 min read

Social media is full of screenshots showing five-figure monthly payouts from prop firms. These screenshots are real in some cases, but they represent the top fraction of a percent of funded traders. If you base your expectations on outliers, you will either be disappointed or take excessive risk trying to match them. This article provides a realistic framework for estimating prop firm income.

The Three Variables That Determine Your Income

Prop firm earnings are a function of three things:

  1. Capital allocated to you. This depends on the firm's pool size and your score relative to other funded traders.
  2. Your trading returns. Expressed as a monthly percentage return on the capital you manage.
  3. Your profit split. The percentage of profits you keep after the firm takes its share.

The formula is simple: Your Income = Capital x Monthly Return x Your Split Percentage

Realistic Monthly Returns

This is where most projections go wrong. Traders assume they will earn 5-10% monthly because that is what they have done on a small personal account during a good stretch. But sustainable, risk-controlled returns on larger capital are lower.

Based on observed data from funded traders across the industry:

  • Conservative (most common): 1-2% monthly on allocated capital
  • Moderate (strong performers): 2-4% monthly
  • Exceptional (top tier): 4-6% monthly, sustained over quarters

Anything above 6% monthly on a sustained basis is extremely rare. Traders who claim higher returns are usually measuring a short window, trading with excessive leverage (which destroys their risk scores), or cherry-picking their best months.

Capital Allocation Scenarios

At Climber Trade, capital allocation depends on the total pool size, your CLIMB Score, and a Cohort Factor that automatically adjusts allocation based on the number of funded traders. Here are illustrative scenarios based on different score tiers:

Standard Tier (Score 85-89)

A Standard tier trader receives a base allocation from the general pool. Assuming a pool of $1,000,000 with moderate cohort size, a score-85 trader might manage approximately $10,000-$15,000 in capital. At 2% monthly return and a 50/50 split, monthly income would be $100-$150.

This is modest, and it is honest. Standard tier is the entry point. The value is not the initial income but the proof of concept and the path to scaling.

Expert Tier (Score 90-94)

An Expert tier trader gets a larger share of the pool plus an evolutionary prime bonus. With the same pool, a score-92 trader might manage $18,000-$30,000. At 3% monthly return and a 55/45 split, monthly income would be $297-$495.

Elite Tier (Score 95-100)

Elite traders access both the general pool (90%) and the Elite pool (10%), plus maximum prime and seniority bonuses. A score-97 trader with 6 months of seniority might manage $40,000-$80,000. At 3% monthly and a 60/40 split, monthly income would be $720-$1,440.

These numbers grow as the pool grows. As more traders subscribe and successful traders generate revenue, the pool increases. Early funded traders benefit disproportionately from this growth because their seniority bonus compounds.

The Scaling Effect

The most important insight about prop firm income is that it is not static. Unlike a salary, your income grows as your score improves, as the pool grows, and as you accumulate seniority. A trader who earns $200 in month one might earn $1,000 in month six and $3,000 in month twelve, all while trading with the same strategy and similar monthly returns.

This scaling is why consistency matters more than short-term profitability. A trader who maintains a score of 90 for 12 months will out-earn one who spikes to 95 for two months and then drops to 80.

What About Costs?

Your net income must account for the evaluation subscription ($199/month for the Standard plan). This continues even after you are funded, as it contributes to the pool. So your first $199 in monthly earnings essentially covers the subscription cost. Beyond that, everything is profit.

You should also consider your own costs: internet, platform fees (if any), market data subscriptions, and tax obligations. Funded trading income is typically classified as professional non-salaried income, and you are responsible for your own tax declarations. The Honest Assessment

Can you make a living from prop firm trading? Yes, but probably not immediately. The path looks like this:

  • Months 1-3: Evaluation phase. No income. Cost: $199/month.
  • Months 4-6: Initial funded phase. Income: $100-$500/month. Covers subscription plus small profit.
  • Months 6-12: Scaling phase. Income: $500-$2,000/month for consistent performers.
  • Year 2+: Established phase. Income: $1,000-$5,000+/month for top-tier traders with seniority.

These ranges assume a single funded account. Some traders manage multiple accounts (one personal, one funded), which diversifies income sources. Others build referral income alongside their trading, creating a compound earnings stream.

Comparing to Alternatives

The question is not whether prop firm income seems high in absolute terms. It is whether it represents a good return on invested time and capital relative to alternatives. A trader spending 4 hours daily during evaluation and funded trading is investing roughly 80 hours per month. If funded trading generates $1,000/month after 6 months, that is $12.50/hour, which is not impressive.

But the growth trajectory changes that math dramatically. If the same trader reaches $3,000/month in year two, $5,000/month in year three, and continues scaling with no ceiling, the hourly rate improves continuously. Combined with the fact that your invested capital is near zero (just the subscription fee), the return on capital invested is essentially infinite.

No other path to professional trading offers this combination of low capital risk and unlimited upside. The trade-off is time and discipline.
