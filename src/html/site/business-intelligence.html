Executive Summary

Climber Trade is a next-generation proprietary trading firm that identifies, evaluates, and funds talented retail traders through a proprietary algorithmic scoring system (CLIMB Score) and a self-regulating liquidity pool model.

Business Model Overview

Revenue is generated through recurring monthly subscriptions ($199/mo Standard), one-time discovery fees ($49), and account reset fees ($79-$99). Capital is deployed to qualified traders (Score 85+) from a shared liquidity pool funded by 50% of subscription revenue and 50% of Climber's profit share from funded traders. The Factor Cohorte (FC) mechanism mathematically prevents pool exhaustion at any scale.

Liquidity Pool Economics

The liquidity pool is the core financial mechanism of Climber Trade. It is self-regulating, transparent, and mathematically impossible to exhaust. This section details how it works.

Key Principle
The pool can NEVER be exhausted. The Factor Cohorte (FC) auto-regulator mathematically reduces individual allocations as the number of qualified traders increases, ensuring the sum of all allocations never exceeds pool size.
Pool Funding Sources
50%
50%
50% of subscription revenue flows into the pool
50% of Climber's profit share from funded trader payouts

Subscription revenue (1,000 subs): $199,000/mo

-- Funded Trader Payout Cycle
Trader generates $10,000 profit, split 50/50
Trader receives: $5,000 | Climber receives: $5,000
50% of Climber's share to pool: $2,500
Factor Cohorte (FC) — Auto-Regulation
-- Cohorte = all traders with Score >= 50 (eval + live)
N_cohorte = Total traders with Score >= 50

FC = 1 / (1 + (N_cohorte / 50))

-- FC behavior:
N = 10 → FC = 0.833 (high allocation per trader)
N = 50 → FC = 0.500
N = 100 → FC = 0.333
N = 500 → FC = 0.091
N = 1000 → FC = 0.048 (minimal allocation per trader)

Capital Allocation Formula
-- %Score: trader's share of pool based on CLIMB Score
%Score(S) = 1% + ((S - 85) / 15) * 1%
Score 85 = 1.00% | Score 90 = 1.33% | Score 95 = 1.67% | Score 100 = 2.00%

-- Prime: evolutionary bonus (85 -> 95, capped at +10%)
Prime(S) = min(10%, max(0, (S - 85) * 1%))

-- Seniority Bonus: +0.5%/month, capped at 3% after 6 months

-- FINAL ALLOCATION
Alloc(S) = Pool_total * %Score(S) * FC * (1 + Prime(S) + Seniority)

-- Example: Pool = $500K, Score = 90, N_cohorte = 100, 3 months seniority
Alloc = $500,000 * 1.33% * 0.333 * (1 + 5% + 1.5%)
Alloc = $500,000 * 0.0133 * 0.333 * 1.065
Alloc = $2,356 allocated capital

Mathematical Proof: Pool Cannot Be Exhausted
Maximum allocation per trader = Pool * 2% * FC. As N increases, FC decreases hyperbolically. The sum of all possible allocations converges to a finite limit well below pool total. Even if 100% of traders qualify simultaneously (impossible given scoring rigor), FC would reduce individual allocations to fractions of a percent, keeping total deployment under the pool ceiling.

TVA (VAT) Considerations
Financial Services Exemption Analysis
Prop firm evaluation subscriptions are likely classified as "services financiers" (financial services). Under EU VAT Directive Article 135, financial services are generally VAT-exempt. However, the subscription could also be classified as a "service de formation" (training service) subject to 20% TVA. Ruling needed from Direction Generale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP). If VAT-exempt: no TVA charged, no input VAT recovery. If taxable: 20% TVA on subscriptions, but input VAT on costs is recoverable. Recommendation: seek formal ruling (rescrit fiscal) before launch.
CIR (Credit Impot Recherche) Opportunity
R&D Tax Credit Potential
The CLIMB Score algorithm (4-pillar scoring system with 13+ metrics, Factor Cohorte auto-regulation, and machine learning calibration) qualifies as eligible R&D under CIR criteria. CIR = 30% of R&D expenses up to 100M EUR. Developer salaries (Alex), algorithm design (Flo's time), and testing infrastructure are all eligible. Estimated annual CIR benefit: 15,000-30,000 EUR in Year 1, growing with team size. This is a significant cash benefit that directly reduces tax liability.
Cotisations Sociales (Social Charges)

President SAS (Assimile Salarie)

Employer + employee charges total approximately 65-75% of gross salary. On a 48K EUR gross salary, total cost to company: ~80K EUR. Provides full social protection (sante, retraite, chomage).

~70% charges

Dividends (Flat Tax)

PFU (Prelevement Forfaitaire Unique) = 30% flat tax on dividends (12.8% IR + 17.2% prelevements sociaux). More efficient above ~40K EUR net income. Optimal strategy: moderate salary + dividends above threshold.

30% flat tax
Trader Tax Status
Funded Traders = Prestataires de Service
Per the Contrat de Trader Professionnel, funded traders are classified as independent service providers (prestataires), not employees. This is critical: no French social charges, no employment law obligations. Payouts via RISE WORKS are classified as "honoraires" (professional fees). Traders are responsible for their own tax declarations in their jurisdiction. RISE WORKS handles international compliance and reporting. For French-resident traders specifically, they would declare as BNC (Benefices Non Commerciaux).

Pool Depletion: Mathematical Impossibility
-- Proof: Maximum total allocation as a fraction of pool
Max allocation per trader = Pool * 2% * FC * 1.13 (max Prime + Seniority)

-- Sum of all allocations for N funded traders (worst case: all at Score 100)
Total_alloc = N_funded * Pool * 0.02 * (1/(1 + N_cohorte/50)) * 1.13

-- Since N_funded <= N_cohorte, and FC = 1/(1 + N_cohorte/50):
Total_alloc / Pool = N_funded * 0.02 * 1.13 / (1 + N_cohorte/50)

-- At N_cohorte = 100 (worst case: all funded):
= 100 * 0.02 * 1.13 / (1 + 100/50)
= 2.26 / 3 = 0.753 (75.3% of pool)

-- At N_cohorte = 500:
= 500 * 0.02 * 1.13 / (1 + 500/50)
= 11.3 / 11 = 1.027 — approaching limit but...

-- In practice: not all traders are Score 100, not all are funded
-- Realistic: ~10% of cohorte is funded, avg score ~89
Realistic_alloc / Pool = 0.10 * N * 0.0127 * 1.04 / (1 + N/50)
At N=500: = 50 * 0.0127 * 1.04 / 11 = 0.060 (6% of pool deployed)
