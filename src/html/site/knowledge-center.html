Section 1

The CLIMB Score

Your single number from 0 to 100 that determines your rank, your funding, and your career progression.

The CLIMB Score is your overall trading rating on a scale of 0 to 100. It measures how well you trade across four dimensions: risk control, profitability, consistency, and execution quality.

Think of it like a credit score, but for trading. The higher your score, the more capital you receive and the better your profit split becomes. It updates in real-time as you trade, with a daily consolidation at end of day.

The first three pillars are averaged together (weighted), and then the Quality Execution pillar acts as a multiplier that can adjust your final score up or down by up to 10%.

In plain English: We take your Risk score, Performance score, and Consistency score, give extra weight to Risk and Performance (x4 each) and slightly less to Consistency (x3), average them together, then multiply by your Quality Execution factor.

Average = [(Risk x 4) + (Performance x 4) + (Consistency x 3)] / 11
Final Score = Average x (1 + ((Quality - 50) / 500))

The denominator is 11 because 4 + 4 + 3 = 11. The Quality pillar creates a multiplier between 0.90 (bad execution) and 1.10 (excellent execution).

Worked example: Risk = 80, Performance = 78, Consistency = 76, Quality = 75.
Average = [(80 x 4) + (78 x 4) + (76 x 3)] / 11 = [320 + 312 + 228] / 11 = 860 / 11 = 78.2
Multiplier = 1 + ((75 - 50) / 500) = 1 + 0.05 = 1.05
Final Score = 78.2 x 1.05 = 82.1

A score from 5 days of trading is not as trustworthy as one from 200 days. That is why we mark your score with a reliability level based on how long you have been trading.

How to Improve

  • Take at least 200 trades to get the full confidence multiplier
  • Even a modest PF of 1.5 with 200+ trades scores well (70 x 1.00 = 70 pts)

This pillar does not have its own weight — instead it adjusts your final score up or down by up to 10%. It rewards clean, balanced execution.

What it measures

Skew measures how symmetric your distribution of returns is. A skew near 0 means your wins and losses are balanced in shape. A high skew (above 2.0) means your returns are lopsided — possibly relying on a few rare big wins to offset many small losses. You start at 100/100 and lose points as skew increases.
